# Canvass QC rules

## Flash knocking
Flash knocking is recording doors faster than a person can walk to them and knock. A shift is flagged when 10 or more consecutive knocks are recorded less than 30 seconds apart. Confirmed flash knocking is rolled back: the knocks in the window no longer count as doors or contacts. A whole shift recorded this way is rolled back in full.

## Householding
Householding is recording results for several units or voters at one address from a single conversation, instead of knocking each unit. A shift is flagged when 4 or more units at the same address are marked canvassed within 5 minutes with identical survey answers. Householded results are not rolled back automatically; the field lead reviews them with the canvasser.

## Gaps
A gap flag is raised when a canvasser is clocked in but records no knocks for 60 minutes or more. Gaps can be legitimate (a long conversation, a turf change, a break that was not clocked out), so the canvasser explains it before any pay adjustment.

## Apartments and units
Every apartment unit is its own door and must be knocked on its own. If a building is locked and the canvasser cannot get in, record the building once as "inaccessible" on its first unit and move on; do not mark each unit. A canvasser who marks a whole building from the lobby is householding.

## What happens after a flag
Every open flag needs a conversation between the field lead and the canvasser before the canvasser's next shift, using the timestamped evidence on the flag. After the conversation the flag is marked reviewed. Two or more householding or flash knocking flags in one week for the same canvasser go to the field director.

## QC rate
The QC rate is flags per 100 GOTV shifts for an office in a week. Week over week trends use this rate, not raw flag counts, because the number of shifts grows during the program.
