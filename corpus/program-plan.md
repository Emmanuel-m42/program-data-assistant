# Riverbend Votes program plan

## Overview
Riverbend Votes is a paid GOTV door canvass run from three offices: Northside, Eastgate and Harbor. Knocking started Monday, September 21, 2026 and runs every day through Election Day, Tuesday, November 3, 2026. Each office has its own turf (a list of precincts) and its own daily door goal that ramps up toward Election Day.

## Goals
The door goal for each office is the sum of its daily goals from September 21 through November 3. Program goal is the sum of the three offices. Daily goals ramp from about 60 percent of the average day at the start to about 150 percent in the final week.

## Pace and projection
Pace compares doors to date with the goal to date. The projection for Election Day assumes nothing changes: doors to date plus the average daily doors of the last 7 days times the knocking days left. An office "will miss goal" when its projection is below its total goal; the shortfall is the goal minus the projection.

## Shifts needed
Shifts per day needed is the doors still needed (total goal minus doors to date) divided by knocking days left, divided by the average doors per shift over the last 7 days. Extra shifts per day is that number minus the average shifts per day over the last 7 days. Field directors use this to set recruitment targets.

## Staff definitions
A regular canvasser has worked 5 or more days in the program. A one-day hire worked exactly one day. An active canvasser worked at least one shift in the last 7 days. These definitions apply to GOTV canvassers.

## More doors or a second pass
When projected capacity for the rest of the program (average daily doors of the last 7 days times days left) is larger than the untouched doors left in our turf, we will run out of first-pass turf. In that case ask the client for more doors first; new turf contacts voters at the normal first-pass rate. A second pass on not-home doors is the fallback, because second passes contact voters at roughly half the first-pass rate. If untouched doors exceed capacity, keep working the turf we have.

## Opening another office
A new office should sit closest to the doors we still need: the untouched doors in our turf, weighted by how many doors each precinct has left. Untouched or lightly touched precincts (under 25 percent of doors touched) that are dense (over 2,000 doors per square mile) are worth a new office; sparse precincts (under 1,000 doors per square mile) are slow to knock and are better handled at the end with drive-out shifts.
